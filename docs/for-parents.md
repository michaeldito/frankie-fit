# Frankie Fit: A Little Explainer for Mom and Dad

## First, what's "Claude"?

Claude is an AI — think of it as a very well-read assistant you can talk to, the same way you'd text a friend. You type a question or describe something you want, and it writes back in plain English. It can read, write, explain things, and even write computer code — the instructions that tell a phone app or a website what to do.

It's not a robot with a body, and it's not "alive" — it doesn't have memories of its own or feelings. It's more like an incredibly capable typewriter that can also think through problems, look things up in what it's learned, and hold a conversation. A few different companies make AIs like this (you may have heard of ChatGPT — that's a similar idea from a different company). Claude is made by a company called Anthropic, and it's the one I've been using to build this project.

## So what is Frankie Fit?

Frankie Fit is a personal health and fitness app I've been building — for myself, but also as a way to learn how to build things using AI.

Here's the everyday version: instead of filling out forms or tapping through a bunch of menus to log your workout, what you ate, how you slept, or how you're feeling, you just tell it — like you're texting a friend who happens to be your personal trainer and nutritionist rolled into one. You could say something like "went for a 30 minute run this morning, felt great" or "had eggs and toast for breakfast," and it figures out what you meant, saves it, and keeps track of it for you.

Over time, it builds up a picture of how you're doing — your workouts, your eating habits, your sleep, your mood — and it can show you your progress, notice patterns, and gently nudge or encourage you based on what it sees. Think of it like a journal that actually organizes itself and can talk back to you.

It works both as an app on your phone and as a website, so you can use whichever is easiest at the moment.

## How did I actually make this?

This is the part that would've sounded like science fiction not too long ago: I built almost all of it by having a conversation.

Instead of learning every little technical detail myself and typing out every line of computer code by hand, I describe what I want in plain English — "I want a screen where someone can log a meal by typing a sentence" — and Claude writes the actual code that makes that happen. I review it, test it, ask for changes, and we go back and forth, the same way you'd work with a contractor building something in your house, except the "contractor" is an AI and the conversation happens in text, often in minutes instead of days.

Behind the scenes there are a few pieces working together: a part you actually see and interact with (the app on your phone or the website), a part that safely stores all your information (like a filing cabinet, but digital and secure), and the AI itself, which reads what you write, makes sense of it, and decides what to do with it — save it, organize it, or respond to you.

None of this makes me a traditional software engineer overnight — but it means I can build something real, thoughtful, and useful by describing my ideas clearly and working closely with the AI to bring them to life. That's honestly the most exciting part of this whole project for me: not just the fitness app itself, but learning how to build things this new way.
